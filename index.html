Public averages describe the field. They don’t always tell you how to beat it.

Terry reviewed 50 high-level Magic Online drafts of Marvel Superheroes and finished with about a 70% match win rate against strong competition.

His core claim: the public data—especially 17Lands averages—often pointed players toward the wrong conclusions. He gained an edge by identifying what the metagame was missing and drafting around cards that were consistently undervalued.

01

The Format’s Main Pillars

Most players focused on two broad strategies:

01 / Tempo

Blue-white flyers

Win through evasive threats and efficient disruption.

02 / Midrange

Bigger creatures

Own the battlefield with size and steady pressure.

Other decks existed—black-white aggro, red-green strength decks, blue-black tempo, and artifact builds—but the biggest gap in the metagame was control.

Because few players drafted true control decks, Terry was often able to assemble:

  • Efficient removal
  • Flexible mana
  • Late-game card advantage
  • Strong matchups against midrange
  • Enough cheap interaction to survive aggro
02

Removal Was the Best Place to Start

Removal was scarcer than usual, so Terry was happy to first-pick or second-pick it. The best removal spells were valuable because they could answer both small aggressive creatures and large midrange threats.

He especially valued cheap interaction because many important creatures in the format had only one or two toughness.

03

Why the Public Data Was Misleading

White appeared to be the best color in aggregate statistics, but Terry believed that was largely because white had an unusually large number of powerful rares. Once rares were removed, its commons and uncommons looked much less dominant.

The read

  • White was heavily overdrafted
  • Blue remained genuinely strong
  • Black was better than players believed
  • Average win rates could hide important context

Best at common & uncommon Blue + Black

04

Black-Red Was a Synergy Deck

Black-red was not simply a pile of individually strong cards. Its best builds created multiple Villains or artifacts in a single turn, then turned that velocity into payoff.

Hydra Assault RobotRobot Domination Avengers Under SiegeHydra Troopers Madame MasqueTo Reign Supreme

Hydra Assault Robot was mediocre by itself, but powerful when the deck could create several Villains quickly. The successful version of black-red played more like an aggressive combo deck than normal midrange.

06

Robot Domination Was Better Than Its Stats

73%Terry’s match win rate with the card

Robot Domination had poor public results, but Terry believed players were putting it in the wrong decks. It needed cheap removal, a stable board, life gain, disposable creatures, and ways to discard, surveil, or cycle creatures into the graveyard.

If cast while ahead or near parity, it could take over the game by replacing creatures as they died and eventually creating three 2/2 tokens.

Especially effective with

  • Cruel Alliance
  • Brave Brawler
  • Undercover Skrull
  • Deathtouch creatures
  • Land cyclers
  • Instant-speed discard or surveil
  • Hydra Assault Robot
  • To Reign Supreme

The card functioned as a powerful source of card advantage in a format where reliable card draw was rare.

07

Other Underrated Cards

Equipment

Hawkeye’s Bow

The Bow was not a generic flyers card. It was best with creatures that could convert tapping or attacking into large bursts of damage. Its strongest pairing was Hawkeye, Young Avenger, where the combination could create a major immediate damage swing.

Artifact

The Ten Rings

Most players treated The Ten Rings as unplayable, but Terry saw it as a late-pick card-advantage engine. It was not broadly powerful, but it could solve the exact problem some control decks had: running out of cards.

08

Common Traits of Terry’s Trophy Decks

Across his successful decks, several patterns repeated. These were low-curve control decks with flexible mana—not slow piles waiting for the late game.

01A high number of noncreature spells
02Cheap removal
03Easy splashes
04Strong rares from multiple colors
0515–16 lands with land cyclers
06Card-advantage engines
07Low curves, even in control decks
08A willingness to play cards that wheeled

Many of his decks were multicolor “soup” decks, often built around a red, black, or blue base with several powerful splash cards. The fixing was good enough that staying two colors was often unnecessary.

09

The condensed version

Final Takeaways

  1. 01Draft the metagame, not just the color rankings.
  2. 02Take efficient removal early.
  3. 03Do not trust aggregate data without understanding why it looks that way.
  4. 04Look for strong cards with bad statistics caused by poor deck construction.
  5. 05Control was underdrafted and well positioned.
  6. 06Black-red required Villain and artifact synergies, not just good cards.
  7. 07Flexible mana made multicolor control decks much better than they first appeared.
  8. 08Late-pick build-arounds could become premium cards in the right shell.

Terry’s edge came from understanding how cards worked together, recognizing where public drafting behavior was distorted, and repeatedly selecting undervalued cards that fit a coherent plan.
